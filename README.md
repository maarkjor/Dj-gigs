GIG BOOK — DJ Calendar
A simple calendar app built for DJs. Track your gigs, venues, working hours, contacts, payment status and more — all in one place, with a clean at-a-glance view so you always know what time slots you have free.

⚠️ Read this before you start adding gigs
This app saves your data directly on your device, inside your browser. This means:

Your data is yours and private — nobody else can see it
It only works on the browser and device where you installed it — if you open the link in a different browser or on a different phone, it will be blank
Do not use it as a casual link in Chrome — your data will not be saved properly

To make it work correctly and keep your gigs saved, you need to install it to your home screen following the steps below.

How to install on iPhone (Safari)

Open the app link in Safari — not Chrome, not any other browser
Tap the Share button at the bottom of the screen (box with arrow pointing up)
Tap "Add to Home Screen"
Give it a name and tap Add

That's it. The app will appear on your home screen like any other app, open fullscreen, and your gigs will be saved and waiting every time you open it.

How to install on Android (Chrome)

Open the app link in Chrome
Tap the three dots menu (top right)
Tap "Add to Home Screen"
Confirm and tap Add


How it works

Tap any date to add one or more gigs
For each gig you can set: venue, working hours, contact, notes, payment status (paid / to be paid) and payment method (cash / bank transfer) and amount.
Choose the gig type yourself — Afternoon, Night, or After Midnight — this sets the color of the dot on the calendar.
Dots appear on each date so you can see at a glance what time slots are taken without opening every day.
Blinking dots mean the gig hasn't been paid yet.
Tap ⚙ COLORS to customize the color for each gig type.
Swipe left or right on the calendar to change month.


Sharing with other DJs
Send them this link. They open it, install it to their home screen, and they get their own blank app. Your data and their data are completely separate — nobody sees each other's gigs.
